Phase 1, core and web. Sign in to a Jellyfin server by address, user and password or with Quick Connect (F1, F2); the token is encrypted in the browser with a key the page cannot export (F5). Home with My songs, Recently played and Made for you in simple mode, plus Recently added and Most played in advanced (F7, F84). Library with artists, album artists, albums, songs, genres, years and playlists in grid and list, sorted and filtered from the URL (F8, F15), all from a local IndexedDB cache with a delta refresh (F14). Album, artist, playlist and favourites pages (F9, F10, F12, F13). Instant library search under the Ask bar with recent searches (F11, F75). Gapless playback through two media elements with direct play or transcoding by network class, a persisted queue with drag reorder, play next, add to end, clear, history, shuffle with an album-aware option, repeat, a now-playing screen, media keys through MediaSession, and play reporting so play counts work everywhere (F20 to F27). Settings with the simple or advanced switch, theme, language, streaming quality per network and sign-out. Every string in English and Gujarati. Mobile gains the mirrored TrackRow and AlbumCard components and the shared icon set; its screens follow in phase 3.

After the first round of review: an artist page shows every song and album whatever id the server holds for the name (Jellyfin keeps two entities for one artist when a library gains artist folders later), and works with the server down; a reload no longer counts as a play or starts a transcode; a token the server revoked sends you to sign-in with a sentence instead of a wall of errors; playlist reorders are read back from Jellyfin and compensated for 10.10's off-by-one, with the toast after the read-back and an undo; new playlists are private to you; every request times out (8 s on sign-in, 15 s elsewhere) with the "could not reach" sentence; the cache fetches what a refresh missed and its delta reads from the server's own item stamps (the newest date added and date saved it has seen), so no clock on either side matters; sign-out removes your library, queue and token from the browser; a scheme-less address tries https first and says so before using http; placeholder art matches the row, the bar fits a tablet, now playing fits a desktop and a phone, and genre, year and placeholder labels read in the light theme.

After the second round: the player bar keeps a usable scrubber at every width from 1024 px in simple mode (times and volume from 1280 px there); placeholder and genre labels render in the one ink that reads on every swatch in the dark theme too; the bar's placeholder colour matches the row's; the sidebar language switch and the Library sync line follow simple mode as the phone does; a browser that blocks IndexedDB (a private window with site data blocked) still signs in and loads the library, kept in memory for the session; counts print with thousands grouping ("20,000 songs", Indian grouping in Gujarati); a scheme-less address that answers on neither scheme says so after two tries instead of three; a sign-out in one tab signs the other tabs of that user out too; a late 401 from a previous user's requests no longer signs the next user out; the reorder planner forgets a learned model that stopped fitting; a favourite reaches the other tabs once the server has it.

After the third round: a heart tapped on an album page fills at once; a heart tapped while the library is refreshing stays after the refresh lands; a favourite or a play made on another device reaches this one the moment the server announces it, for any song in the library, not only the 200 most recently saved; the player bar keeps its five transport buttons on one row on tablets in simple mode; the whole Ask bar box takes a tap; switching to Gujarati changes the page language and the words together, and a switch whose strings cannot be fetched leaves everything as it was and says so; a screen that cannot load offline says you are offline and loads itself when the connection returns; a reload no longer flashes "no favourites" or "this playlist is gone" while the cache loads from disk; an artist or album named exactly what you typed leads the instant results; Settings names the full rebuild "Rebuild library" apart from the quick refresh; a song whose name is only spaces shows "(untitled)".

After the fourth round: the current row shows the pause glyph while the song plays and the play glyph while it is paused, as the bar does; a song's menu opens above its button when the list has no room below it, so every item is reachable on the last rows of a phone; the song, album and artist counts on the Library and Settings pages wait for the library to load instead of saying 0 for a moment, and Home shows no empty sentence while the session is still being read; a favourite or a play pushed from another device is kept even when one entry of the push is malformed, the unknown items are fetched in one request and only music is kept; a playlist deleted during a refresh stays deleted after a restart; the album page keeps the later of the two last-played dates when one side has none. Rework round 5: the queue is saved as song ids, so a shuffle of a whole library survives a reload and a save that fails is said once; a stream that fails plays again from Play and never spreads to the next song; play reports go out in order; untitled files are named in the user's language; the library says when its first load failed instead of claiming a last time, hides counts until the first sync is in, and runs a rebuild asked for during a refresh; add to an empty queue says "Playing"; Settings captions its theme, language and network choices; album and artist pages say "Loading…" while they look a missing item up; the year filter carries the filter glyph; row menus follow their row on scroll. Rework round 6: the queue page and the playlist page draw only the rows in view, so a shuffle of a whole library or a 3,000-song playlist opens and acts at once; every screen names itself in the tab and the history ("Library · Renaissance", an album by its title), a navigation moves focus into the new screen, and the not-found page has a heading; Favourites and Search signed out show the same sentence and Sign in as the Library; the phone header drops the name when the type is too large for it; signing out stops the library refresh in flight instead of letting it run on a revoked token; the playback position is saved under its own key every five seconds while the queue is written only when it changes, and a signed-out user's queue is never written back; an album or artist the server could not be reached for says so and is asked again, with "not on the server any more" kept for a real not-found; the browser suite deletes the device records it signed in with.
